Vortex lattice method with tip shedding

Tip Shedding Revision

Verification that the tip-loading issue of the internship report is overcome, and a record of every change made to the original code.

Reference

Rectangular wing, aspect ratio one, incidence 10°, both tips shedding.
Date
4 September 2026

1 Summary

The internship report observes that, for a rectangular wing with tip shedding, the sectional lift coefficient does not fall towards the tip and depends on the cutoff distance of the Biot–Savart law. A cutoff larger than half the smallest panel width gives a rounded distribution but lowers the lift by 30 % (report, Fig. 3.3). The report attributes this to the singular velocity induced by nearby vortex segments.

The behaviour is numerical and has three causes in the formulation, none of which is a programming error. First, the rings shed from the tips carried the circulation of the previous time step, so the side-edge Kutta condition was satisfied with a one-step lag and the tip circulation relaxed at a rate proportional to the tip panel width. Second, the influence matrix and the right-hand side used different cutoffs, so that raising the cutoff switched the tip shedding off rather than smoothing the result. Third, the wake roll-up velocity was singular within a fraction of the tip panel width.

The revised method imposes the Kutta condition implicitly, uses one cutoff for the boundary condition, evaluates the lift with the linearised Kutta–Joukowski theorem and regularises the wake convection with a vortex core. For the report's test case the sectional lift is independent of the regularisation parameter, converges with the spanwise mesh and with the time step, and its tip value changes by less than 1 % between 30 and 80 spanwise panels (Sect. 5). The lift coefficient is 0.386, against 0.384 before the revision, 0.37 for the vortex lattice method of Rodriguez and 0.33 for the experiment of Bartlett and Vidal. The finite loading at the tip remains. It is the lift of the side-edge vortex sheet that the tip shedding is designed to represent, and it no longer carries a mesh-dependent spike.

2 The issue

The report states that the Biot–Savart law "can produce huge velocities when the point of computation is really close to the segment, especially at the tip", that this "can also induce a distribution that does not drop to zero at the tip", and that a cutoff distance \(d\) larger than \(0.5\,b_\mathrm{min}\), where \(b_\mathrm{min}\) is the smallest panel width, gives "a more physical distribution shape" but "a less precise global model" (report, p. 20). Figure 3.3 of the report shows the two distributions for a rectangular wing of aspect ratio one at 10° incidence.

Figure 1(a) reproduces both curves with the original code. With \(d = 0.4\,b_\mathrm{min}\) the sectional lift coefficient \(C_l\) is 0.405 at the tip strip and 0.36 at its minimum; with \(d = 0.6\,b_\mathrm{min}\) it falls to 0.07 at the tip and the lift coefficient \(C_L\) drops from 0.384 to 0.267. The distribution with the larger cutoff is within 3 % of the one obtained with tip shedding disabled (\(C_L = 0.260\)), which is the first indication that the cutoff was not smoothing the tip vortex but removing it.

3 Diagnosis

3.1 Where the tip lift comes from

Table 1 splits the sectional lift of the tip strip in the original formulation into the contributions of the vortex elements that induce velocity at the bound segments. Two contributions of about 0.74 nearly cancel: the chordwise tip-edge segments of the wing rings, of strength \(-\Gamma\), and the edge of the shed tip ring that lies on the same line, of strength \(+\Gamma\) of the previous step. Both are half a tip panel width from the bound-segment midpoint, so each scales with \(\Gamma / b_\mathrm{min}\). The residual of the cancellation is small, and the remaining excess of 0.03 over the neighbouring strips comes from the rolled-up tip filaments. The free-stream term alone, which is twice the bound circulation, is already 0.382 at the tip. The loading does not fall towards the tip because the circulation does not, and not because of the load evaluation.

3.2 The lagged side-edge Kutta condition

The two large terms of Table 1 also appear in the boundary condition at the tip control point: the wing edge in the influence matrix, the shed edge in the right-hand side. Writing the circulation of the tip panel at step \(n\) as \(\Gamma^{n}\) and the singular coefficient as \(K / b_\mathrm{min}\), the boundary condition at the tip reads

\[ \frac{K}{b_\mathrm{min}}\left(\Gamma^{n} - \Gamma^{n-1}\right) = F\left(\Gamma^{n}, \text{wake}\right), \]

where \(F\) collects the finite terms. The tip circulation therefore advances by \(b_\mathrm{min} F / K\) per step. The finer the tip panel, the slower the approach to the steady state, and the steady state is reached only when \(F = 0\). Figure 4(a) confirms this. With 30 spanwise panels the lift coefficient reaches 97 % of its final value after three chord lengths of travel; with 80 panels, where the tip panel is 0.4 mm wide on a span of 1 m, it reaches 86 % and is still rising at eight chord lengths. At a fixed simulation length the results appear to diverge under mesh refinement (Fig. 2a), although they would converge given enough time.

3.3 The cutoff and the boundary condition

The original code applied one cutoff to the influence matrix and another to the right-hand side and to the loads. Raising the second above \(0.5\,b_\mathrm{min}\) removes the shed edge from the boundary condition at the tip control point while the wing edge stays in the matrix. The tip panel then feels its own edge vorticity without cancellation, its circulation collapses, and the solution reverts to the one without tip shedding. This is what Fig. 3.3(b) of the report shows. The rounded shape is not more physical; it is the tip shedding switched off by an inconsistent regularisation.

3.4 The singular roll-up velocity

The wake corner points were convected with a cutoff of \(0.4\,b_\mathrm{min}\), which is 1 mm at 30 spanwise panels and 0.2 mm at 80. Filaments of the rolled-up tip sheet that pass within a few millimetres of a bound segment or a control point induce velocities of the order of the free stream. Their effect grows as the time step shrinks, because the sheet is then discretised into more, shorter elements near the tip. In the original formulation the tip \(C_l\) at 50 spanwise panels goes from 0.42 to −0.21 when \(u_\infty \Delta t / c\) goes from 0.1 to 0.025 (Fig. 3a).

4 Changes to the method

Four changes address the three causes. They are listed with the section of Katz and Plotkin, Low-Speed Aerodynamics, that they follow where applicable.

  1. Implicit Kutta condition at the trailing edge and at the tips. The rings shed during the current step carry the current circulation of the panels they are shed from, and their influence is part of the influence matrix (Katz and Plotkin, Sect. 13.12). The wing tip-edge vorticity and the shed edge now cancel exactly at every step. The geometry of the newly shed rings is fixed for a constant time step, so the matrix is still inverted once.
  2. One cutoff for the boundary condition. The influence matrix and the right-hand side share the cutoff \(d = 0.4\,b_\mathrm{min}\), which must stay below \(0.5\,b_\mathrm{min}\) so that no control point lies inside the cutoff of its own ring.
  3. Linearised Kutta–Joukowski theorem for the lift. The load on a segment is \(\rho\,\boldsymbol{u}_\infty \times \boldsymbol{\Gamma}\) (Katz and Plotkin, Eq. 12.25). The velocity induced by a discrete lattice at a segment midpoint is of order \(\Gamma / b\) and does not converge under refinement, so it is no longer used for the lift. It is kept in a separate induced-load vector that carries the induced drag.
  4. Scully vortex core for the wake convection. The velocity used for the wake roll-up and for the induced loads is multiplied by \(h^2 / (h^2 + r_\mathrm{c}^2)\), where \(h\) is the distance to the segment and \(r_\mathrm{c} = 0.05\,c\). The core radius is a physical length, independent of the mesh. The results vary by ca. 1 % for \(r_\mathrm{c}/c\) between 0.02 and 0.1 (Fig. 1b).

5 Verification

The test case is the one of Fig. 3.3 of the report: a rectangular wing of aspect ratio one at \(\alpha = 10°\), five chordwise panels, cosine spanwise spacing, wake shed from the trailing edge and from both tips, uniform time step, and a simulation length of three chord lengths of travel unless stated. \(N\) denotes the total number of spanwise panels. Every case was run with the original code, kept as VLMSolver_original.py, and with the revised code. Table 2 gathers the tip value of the sectional lift and the lift coefficient.

5.1 Independence of the regularisation parameter

In the revised formulation the boundary condition contains no adjustable regularisation other than the cutoff \(d = 0.4\,b_\mathrm{min}\), whose value is immaterial below \(0.5\,b_\mathrm{min}\) because no other vortex element lies closer to a control point than its own ring. The only free parameter is the core radius of the wake convection. Figure 1(b) shows that a factor of five on \(r_\mathrm{c}\) moves the tip \(C_l\) from 0.393 to 0.369 and the lift coefficient from 0.387 to 0.381, against a change from 0.405 to 0.068 and from 0.384 to 0.267 for a factor of 1.5 on the original cutoff.

5.2 Convergence with the spanwise mesh

Figure 2 shows the outer half-span for 16 to 80 spanwise panels. In the original formulation the tip strip rises from 0.40 to 0.49 while the rest of the distribution sinks, and the lift coefficient falls from 0.386 to 0.341. In the revised formulation the four curves lie on top of one another; the tip value moves from 0.382 to 0.390 and the lift coefficient from 0.386 to 0.388. With uniform spacing the revised lift coefficient converges towards the same value, 0.384 at 80 panels.

5.3 Convergence with the time step

Figure 3 shows the tip sectional lift for \(u_\infty \Delta t / c\) = 0.1, 0.05 and 0.025. The original values scatter between −0.21 and 0.42. The revised values lie between 0.374 and 0.388 for both meshes, and the two finer time steps agree to 0.3 %. A time step of \(0.05\,c/u_\infty\) or smaller is recommended; the report's value of \(0.1\,c/u_\infty\) overestimates the tip \(C_l\) by 3 % and the lift coefficient by 1 %.

5.4 Approach to the steady state

Figure 4 shows the lift coefficient against the distance travelled. In the original formulation the fine mesh lags the coarse one by several chord lengths, as predicted in Sect. 3.2. In the revised formulation the two meshes follow the same curve and are within 1 % of their final value after three chord lengths. The value at eight chord lengths is 0.389 for 30 panels and 0.390 for 80.

5.5 Other checks

  • Without tip shedding the revised and original formulations agree to 1 % (\(C_L\) = 0.262 and 0.260), so the changes leave the trailing-edge wake model unchanged in practice.
  • A half-wing in the vertical plane with a wall image gives 99 % of the lift of the corresponding full wing, so the image method is unaffected.
  • The three notebooks of the repository execute without error.

6 Interpretation and consequences

The sectional lift of the revised method is flat across the span, with a shallow minimum at \(|y|/b \approx 0.4\) and a value at the tip within 3 % of the mid-span value. This is the expected result of a side-edge shedding model: the tip panel no longer feels its own edge vorticity, and the side-edge vortex sheet carries the bound circulation to the tip. The classical fall of the loading to zero at the tip belongs to the attached-flow model, which the same code recovers when tip shedding is disabled (\(C_L = 0.26\)). Which of the two is closer to the experiment depends on whether the side edge separates; for this thin plate at 10° the measured lift, 0.33, lies between them.

The coupling algorithm of the report feeds the sectional lift into the ventilation model through the effective angle of attack of each section. In the original formulation the tip sections carried an error of the order of the lift itself, whose sign depended on the time step. In the revised formulation the sectional lift is converged to better than 1 % over the span, so the coupling near the free surface and near the tip rests on a reliable input.

One item remains open. The pressure-difference load routine, which the report does not use for its results, gives a lift coefficient of 0.235 against 0.39 from the Kutta–Joukowski theorem on the same solution. It now runs, but its formulation disagrees with the other method by 40 % and should not be used until that is resolved.

7 Complete list of changes to the original code

The original code is the commit "feat : update and test case" of 3 September 2026. Items 1 to 4 change the method and are described in Sect. 4. Items 5 to 12 correct programming errors found in the review that preceded this study; none of them changes the results of a run that completed. Items 13 to 16 concern documentation and reproducibility.

  1. Implicit Kutta condition: the rings shed at the current step, from the trailing edge and from the tips, carry the current circulation and are part of the influence matrix. The matrix is rebuilt only when the time step changes, which matters for the cosine time-step law.
  2. The influence matrix and the right-hand side share one cutoff distance.
  3. Lift from the linearised Kutta–Joukowski theorem; the induced-velocity load is stored separately as the induced drag.
  4. Scully vortex core, radius 5 % of the mean chord by default, for the wake roll-up and for the induced loads, in place of the hard cutoff. The meaning of the solver's ratio parameter changes accordingly, from a cutoff in units of the smallest panel width to a core radius in units of the mean chord.
  5. The pressure-difference load routine called the induced-velocity kernel with one argument missing and failed on every call; the argument is supplied and the debugging output removed.
  6. A panel of zero area now raises an explicit error instead of failing on an undefined normal.
  7. An unknown time-step law now raises an explicit error instead of failing on an undefined variable.
  8. The wing construction can be called more than once on the same surface; previously the second call doubled the spanwise panel count and failed.
  9. The lifting-surface module imports the panel module in a way that works both from a script and from the notebooks; previously it worked only when the repository root was on the search path.
  10. The first solve without a wake checks every surface, not only the first.
  11. The minimum panel width is initialised to infinity instead of 100 m; an unused import, an unused variable and a shadowed argument are removed; a mislabelled comment on the right tip is corrected.
  12. The last cell of the unsteady-wake notebook, which called an interface that no longer existed, is rewritten as a comparison of the two load computations against simulation length.
  13. Package marker, requirements file and running instructions added to the repository.
  14. Notebook parameter comments and README updated for the new meaning of the regularisation parameters.
  15. This study, its scripts, its figures and a copy of the original solver added under docs/tip_loading.
  16. Recommended settings: \(u_\infty \Delta t / c \le 0.05\), \(r_\mathrm{c}/c = 0.05\), cutoff \(0.4\,b_\mathrm{min}\) for the boundary condition.

8 Reproducing the study

From the repository root, with the packages of requirements.txt installed:

python docs/tip_loading/study.py     # runs both formulations, writes study.json and tsweep.json (ca. 3 min)
python docs/tip_loading/plots.py     # writes fig1 to fig4

Files: study.py (test matrix), plots.py (figures), VLMSolver_original.py (solver before the revision), study.json and tsweep.json (results), report.html (this document).
